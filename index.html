Theorem 3.5 (1.6) (Universal surface series)

Statement

Let S=\mathbb C^2 with weights \lambda_1,\lambda_2, E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^N\mathcal O_S\langle y_j\rangle, and \alpha\in K_{\mathsf T}(S) of rank r\in\mathbb Z. Write \mathcal S_S,\mathcal C_S,\mathcal V_S for the equivariant virtual Segre, Chern, and Verlinde series on \operatorname{Quot}_S(E,n).

For partitions \mu,\nu,\xi, set c_\mu(\beta)=\prod_i c_{\mu_i}(\beta) and

I_{\mu,\nu,\xi}=\int_S c_\mu(\alpha)c_\nu(T_S)c_\xi(E)c_1(T_S).

For every integer r, there exist universal series A_{\mu,\nu,\xi},B_{\mu,\nu,\xi},C_{\mu,\nu,\xi}\in1+q\mathbb Q[\![q]\!], depending only on the ranks of E and \alpha, such that

\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S_S(E,\alpha;q)&=\prod_{\mu,\nu,\xi}A_{\mu,\nu,\xi}(q)^{I_{\mu,\nu,\xi}},\\
\mathcal V_S(E,\alpha;q)&=\prod_{\mu,\nu,\xi}B_{\mu,\nu,\xi}(q)^{I_{\mu,\nu,\xi}},\\
\mathcal C_S(E,\alpha;q)&=\prod_{\mu,\nu,\xi}C_{\mu,\nu,\xi}(q)^{I_{\mu,\nu,\xi}}.
\end{aligned}

Explanation

The equivariant virtual Segre, Chern, and Verlinde invariants are expressed by universal series. This structured allows easier local computation of those invariants in general.

Theorem 3.11 (1.7) (Reduced series are logarithms)

Statement

The rank-dependent universal series A,B,C\in1+q\mathbb Q[\![q]\!] are defined by

\log\mathcal U=\sum_{\mu,\nu,\xi}I_{\mu,\nu,\xi}\log D_{\mu,\nu,\xi},\quad(\mathcal U,D)=(\mathcal S_S,A),(\mathcal V_S,B),(\mathcal C_S,C).

Consider the reduced obstruction theory obtained by removing one trivial obstruction direction. Put

J_{\mu,\nu,\xi}=\left.\int_Sc_\mu(\alpha)c_\nu(T_S)c_\xi(E)\right|_{\lambda_2=-\lambda_1}.

Then

\begin{aligned}
\mathcal S^{\mathrm{red}}(E,\alpha;q)&=\sum_{\mu,\nu,\xi}\log A_{\mu,\nu,\xi}(q)\,J_{\mu,\nu,\xi},\\
\mathcal V^{\mathrm{red}}(E,\alpha;q)&=\sum_{\mu,\nu,\xi}\log B_{\mu,\nu,\xi}(q)\,J_{\mu,\nu,\xi},\\
\mathcal C^{\mathrm{red}}(E,\alpha;q)&=\sum_{\mu,\nu,\xi}\log C_{\mu,\nu,\xi}(q)\,J_{\mu,\nu,\xi}.
\end{aligned}

Explanation

The same universal functions give the ordinary and reduced invariants. This gives a way to compute reduced invariants.

Theorem 4.3 (1.9) (Explicit higher-degree coefficients)

Statement

For k_1,k_2\ge0, put k=k_1+k_2 and

\begin{aligned}
L_A^{k_1,k_2}&=k_1!k_2!\sum_{|\mu|=k_1,|\xi|=k_2}\binom r\mu\binom N\xi\log A_{\mu,(0),\xi}(q),\\
L_B^{k_1,k_2}&=k_1!k_2!r^{k_1}\sum_{|\xi|=k_2}\binom N\xi\log B_{(1)_{k_1},(0),\xi}(q),\\
L_C^{k_1,k_2}&=k_1!k_2!\sum_{|\mu|=k_1,|\xi|=k_2}\binom r\mu\binom N\xi\log C_{\mu,(0),\xi}(q).
\end{aligned}

Assume r\ge0. For k\ge1,

\begin{aligned}
L_A^{k_1,k_2}&=-r\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-n(r+N))_{(k-1)}}n\binom{-nr-1}{nN-1}q^n,\\
L_B^{k_1,k_2}&=-r^k\sum_{n\ge1}n^{k-2}\binom{-nr-1}{nN-1}((-1)^Nq)^n,\\
L_C^{k_1,k_2}&=r\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(n(r-N))_{(k-1)}}n\binom{nr-1}{nN-1}q^n,
\end{aligned}

where (a)_{(j)}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-j+1) and (a)_{(0)}=1.

For k=0, the formulas are

\begin{aligned}
L_A^{0,0}&=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^2}\binom{-nr}{nN-1}q^n,\\
L_B^{0,0}&=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-1)^{nN-1}}{n^2}\binom{-nr-1}{nN-1}q^n,\\
L_C^{0,0}&=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^2}\binom{nr}{nN-1}q^n.
\end{aligned}

Explanation

These formulas compute explicit relations between Segre, Chern, and Verlinde invariants beyond degree zero.

Theorem 4.6 (1.11) (A formula through formal roots)

Statement

Assume N,r>0 and k\ge1. Set

\varphi(t)=\log(1+t),\qquad\psi(t)=\frac Nt+\frac r{1+t},\qquad D_\psi=\psi(t)^{-1}\frac d{dt},

D_{\mathcal S}^{(k)}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}\bigl(-(r+N)D_\psi-j\bigr),\qquad D_{\mathcal V}^{(k)}=r^{k-1}D_\psi^{k-1},

Let H_1,\ldots,H_N be the N formal roots tending to zero, expanded in powers of q^{1/N}, of

H_i^N=q(1+H_i)^{-r}.

Then

\begin{aligned}
L_A^{k_1,k_2}(q)&=-r\sum_{i=1}^N(D_{\mathcal S}^{(k)}\varphi)(H_i),\\
L_B^{k_1,k_2}((-1)^Nq)&=-r\sum_{i=1}^N(D_{\mathcal V}^{(k)}\varphi)(H_i).
\end{aligned}

Explanation

Both invariants are given by \log(1+t) evaluated at the same formal roots. The Segre and Verlinde coefficients in each degree are related by explicit differential operators.

Corollary 1.4 (The Chern series of a line bundle)

Statement

Let S=\mathbb C^2 with tangent weights \lambda_1,\lambda_2, and let L=\mathcal O_S\langle v\rangle be an equivariant line bundle with w=c_1(L). Let L^{[n]} denote its tautological bundle on \operatorname{Hilb}^n(S). The virtual Chern series, defined by equivariant localization using the standard surface virtual class, satisfies

\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_S(\mathcal O_S,L;q)&:=\sum_{n\ge0}q^n\int_{[\operatorname{Hilb}^n(S)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}c(L^{[n]})\\
&=(1-q)^{-\int_Sc(L)c_1(T_S)}\\
&=(1-q)^{-\frac{(1+w)(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)}{\lambda_1\lambda_2}}.
\end{aligned}

Proposition 5.3 (1.1) (An integral virtual structure sheaf)

Statement

Let X=\mathbb C^4, E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^N\mathcal O_X\langle y_j\rangle with N>0, and M_n=\operatorname{Quot}_X(E,n) for n\ge0. Brackets denote torus characters; the coordinate torus satisfies t_1t_2t_3t_4=1.

For any orientation, let \widehat{\mathcal O}^{\mathrm{vir}} be the twisted virtual structure sheaf of the fourfold obstruction theory, and let (E^\vee)^{[n]} be the tautological class on M_n. Then the untwisted virtual structure sheaf is integral:

\mathcal O^{\mathrm{vir}}:=\widehat{\mathcal O}^{\mathrm{vir}}\otimes\det((E^\vee)^{[n]})^{1/2}\in K_0(M_n,\mathbb Z).

Explanation

The fourfold Verlinde invariants can therefore use an ordinary integral K-theory class, despite the square roots appearing in the twisted construction.

Conjecture 1.18

Conjecture 1.20 (Vanishing below the rank threshold)

Statement

Let Y=\mathbb C^d for d\in\{2,4\}, with tangent weights \lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d, imposing \sum_i\lambda_i=0 when d=4. Let

E=\bigoplus_{j=1}^N\mathcal O_Y\langle y_j\rangle,\qquad N>1,\qquad V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathcal O_Y\langle v_i\rangle.

Theorem 1.5

Statement

Let n\ge2, let p\le n be the largest prime, and let S_n act on \mathbb R^n by permuting coordinates. If v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n) has pairwise distinct coordinates and \sum_i v_i\ne0, then every hyperplane H\subset\mathbb R^n through the origin satisfies

|H\cap S_nv|\le\frac{n!}{p}.

For n\ge3, the maximum over all such v and H equals (n-1)! when n is prime, and n(n-2)! when n is even and n-1 is prime.

Explanation

At most a fraction 1/p of the permutation orbit can lie in a hyperplane. The bound gives the exact extremum whenever n or n-1 is prime.

Proposition 1.6

Statement

For every n\ge2, there is a nonempty Zariski open set U\subset\mathbb R^n, consisting of vectors with pairwise distinct coordinates and nonzero coordinate sum, such that every v\in U satisfies

\max_{H\subset\mathbb R^n\text{ a hyperplane through }0}|H\cap S_nv|=(n-1)!.

Explanation

For a generic vector, the largest hyperplane section contains exactly a fraction 1/n of its permutation orbit. Larger intersections can occur only for vectors in a proper algebraic subset.

Conjecture 1.2

Statement

For n\ge3, let S_n act on \mathbb R^n by permuting coordinates, and define

M_n=\max_{v,H}|H\cap S_nv|,

where v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n) ranges over vectors with pairwise distinct coordinates and \sum_i v_i\ne0, and H ranges over hyperplanes through the origin. We conjecture that

M_n=\begin{cases}(n-1)!,&n\text{ odd},\\n(n-2)!,&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}

Theorem A (Universal non-negativity)

Statement

Let (n,p,q)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^3, and let Y range over complex projective Gorenstein canonical n-folds. Write E_{\mathrm{st}}(Y;u,v)=\sum_{a,b\ge0}(-1)^{a+b}h_{\mathrm{st}}^{a,b}(Y)u^av^b. Then h_{\mathrm{st}}^{p,q}(Y)\ge0 for every Y if and only if at least one of the following holds:

\begin{gathered}n\le2;\qquad\min(p,q)\le1;\qquad(p,q)=(2,2);\\|p-q|\ge n-1;\qquad\{p,q\}=\{2,n\}.\end{gathered}

Explanation

This determines precisely for which (p,q,n) do we have non-negativity of h^{p,q}_\mathrm{st}.

Theorem B (Polynomial stringy E-functions)

Statement

Assume additionally that E_{\mathrm{st}}(Y;u,v) is a polynomial. Then h_{\mathrm{st}}^{p,q}(Y)\ge0 for every such Y if and only if at least one of the following holds:

\begin{gathered}\min(p,q)\le1;\qquad\max(p,q)\ge n-1;\\(p,q)=(2,2);\qquad(p,q)=(n-2,n-2).\end{gathered}

Theorem C (The dimension threshold)

Statement

In the polynomial case, all stringy Hodge numbers are non-negative for n\le4. For every n\ge5, there is such an n-fold Y with h_{\mathrm{st}}^{p,q}(Y)<0 for some p,q\ge0.

Explanation

This settles Batyrev’s non-negativity conjecture in all dimensions.

Theorem A (Stabilization)

Statement

Let \mathcal X/\mathbb C be a smooth equidimensional finite type Artin stack with affine diagonal and a properly stable good moduli space; put d=\dim\mathcal X.

Let \mathcal A_{\mathcal X} be the twisted arcs lifting to the canonical reduction of stabilizers, with weight function \operatorname{wt}_{\mathcal X}. Put A^w=\mathcal A_{\mathcal X}\cap\operatorname{wt}_{\mathcal X}^{-1}(w), and let Y_n^w be the canonical disjoint union of locally closed substacks representing its n-jet image.

For every w\in\mathbb Q and i\in\mathbb Z, there is m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0} such that, for all n\ge m,

H_c^{i+2nd}(Y_n^w)(nd)\cong H_c^{i+2md}(Y_m^w)(md).

Here H_c is compactly supported rational cohomology and (a) is a Tate twist. Denote this stable polarizable mixed Hodge structure by H_{\mathrm{str}}^{i,w}(\mathcal X).

Explanation

This shows our stringy cohomology is well defined.

Theorem B (Hodge realization)

Statement

For \mathcal X as in Theorem A, let \nu_{\mathcal X} be motivic measure, \mathbb L=[\mathbb A^1], and \chi_{\mathrm{Hdg}} and \operatorname{HD} the Hodge and Hodge–Deligne realizations. The stringy class is \mathrm e_{\mathrm{str}}(\mathcal X)=\mathbb L^d\int_{\mathcal A_{\mathcal X}}\mathbb L^{-\operatorname{wt}_{\mathcal X}}\,d\nu_{\mathcal X}.

For i\in\mathbb Q, split

\bigoplus_{w\in\mathbb Q:\,i+2w\in\mathbb Z}H_{\mathrm{str}}^{i+2w,w}(\mathcal X)(w)

into H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{even},i} and H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{odd},i} according to the parity of i+2w, and denote their sum by H_{\mathrm{str}}^i.

For every w\in\mathbb Q,

\chi_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(\mathbb L^d\nu_{\mathcal X}(A^w))=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^j[H_{\mathrm{str}}^{j,w}(\mathcal X)].

Consequently,

\operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(\mathcal X)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Q}\left(\operatorname{HD}(H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{even},i}(\mathcal X))-\operatorname{HD}(H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{odd},i}(\mathcal X))\right).

If this series has only integer exponents, then

\chi_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(\mathrm e_{\mathrm{str}}(\mathcal X))=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^i[H_{\mathrm{str}}^i(\mathcal X)],

\operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(\mathcal X)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^i\operatorname{HD}(H_{\mathrm{str}}^i(\mathcal X)).

The class sums converge in the completed Grothendieck ring of polarizable mixed Hodge structures.

Explanation

This shows our stringy cohomology retrieves the stringy E-function.

Corollary C (Stringy Hodge numbers from cohomology)

Statement

Let Y/\mathbb C be an irreducible log-terminal finite type scheme, and let \mathcal X\to Y be a crepant resolution by a smooth irreducible finite type Artin stack with affine diagonal. Then

\operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(Y)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Q}\left(\operatorname{HD}(H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{even},i}(\mathcal X))-\operatorname{HD}(H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{odd},i}(\mathcal X))\right).

If \operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(Y) has only integer exponents, for example if Y is 1-Gorenstein, then

\operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(Y)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^i\operatorname{HD}(H_{\mathrm{str}}^i(\mathcal X)).

Theorem D (Orbifold comparison)

Statement

Let \mathcal X/\mathbb C be a smooth equidimensional tame finite type Artin stack with affine diagonal, and let H_{\mathrm{orb}} be its compactly supported orbifold cohomology, with even/odd parity taken before the age shift.

For every i\in\mathbb Q,

H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{even},i}(\mathcal X)\cong H_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\mathrm{even},i}(\mathcal X),\qquad H_{\mathrm{str}}^{\mathrm{odd},i}(\mathcal X)\cong H_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\mathrm{odd},i}(\mathcal X).

In particular,

H_{\mathrm{str}}^i(\mathcal X)\cong H_{\mathrm{orb}}^i(\mathcal X),\qquad\operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(\mathcal X)=\operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{orb}}(\mathcal X).

Explanation

For Deligne-Mumford stacks, stringy cohomology recovers orbifold cohomology, with the same age shifts and Tate twists.

Proposition 4.1

Statement

For \mathcal X as in Theorem A, if \operatorname{HD}_{\mathrm{str}}(\mathcal X) has only integer exponents, then

\operatorname{wt}_{\mathcal X}(\mathcal A_{\mathcal X})\subset\mathbb Z.

Explanation

This is the converse to the statement that integral weights (Gorenstein property of singularity) give integral exponents on the stirngy E-function.

Theorem A

Statement

Let f=h+F:(\mathbb C^n,0)\to(\mathbb C,0), where n\ge3, h is homogeneous of degree d\ge2, and F vanishes to order greater than d. Suppose 2\le d<n/2 or d>2n-2.

Let

\mathcal X_m=\left\{\gamma(t)=\sum_{a=1}^{m}\gamma_a t^a:\gamma_a\in\mathbb C^n,\ f(\gamma(t))\equiv t^m\pmod{t^{m+1}}\right\}

be the restricted contact locus, and let \phi be the Milnor monodromy of f. For every j\in\mathbb Z,

H_c^{j+(n-1)(2m+1)}(\mathcal X_m;\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{HF}^{j}(\phi^{m},+;\mathbb Z).

Explanation

The arc–Floer conjecture holds in these degree ranges. This is the first family of positive results for the conjecture in infinitely many dimensions.

Theorem B

Statement

Let f=h+F:(\mathbb C^n,0)\to(\mathbb C,0), where n\ge3, h is homogeneous of degree d\ge2, and F vanishes to order greater than d. Consider the unrestricted contact locus

\mathcal C_m=\{\gamma\in(t\mathbb C\llbracket t\rrbracket)^n:\operatorname{ord}_t f(\gamma)=m\}.

The numbers of dlt, contact and essential valuations are

\begin{array}{c|ccc}&\mathrm{dlt}&\mathrm{contact}&\mathrm{essential}\\\hline d<n&0&\begin{cases}0&m<d\\1&m\ge d\end{cases}&\lfloor m/d\rfloor\\ d\ge n&\lfloor m/d\rfloor&\lfloor m/d\rfloor&\lfloor m/d\rfloor\end{array}

When d\ge n, the three sets of valuations coincide.

Explanation

This resolves the embedded Nash problem for all semihomogeneous singularities.
